02 / How a lesson works

Question first, theory last.

Each lesson is laid out in the order an interview goes. Mark it complete and the list on the left keeps your place on this device.

  1. The 30-second answerWhat to say first, in words you can say aloud.
  2. The follow-upsThe questions an interviewer asks next, each with a model answer to open after you’ve tried.
  3. Hands-onCommands to copy against a POC in the repository, with the output they really print.
  4. Predict, then checkChange one input, write down what you expect, and run it.
  5. Your storyTwo or three sentences that turn what you just ran into “here’s what I built and found”.
  6. Background, on demandThe chapter’s argument, diagrams and case studies, folded away until you want them.

Practise SRE interviews by answering, running and explaining

This course walks you through four books, one lesson per chapter: all 23 chapters of Site Reliability Engineering, 2nd Edition, the 10 chapters published so far of Platform Engineering at Google, and the two newest observability books on O’Reilly, Mastering Prometheus (2024) and Observability with Grafana (2024), 15 chapters each. I’m your tutor: each lesson explains the ideas in plain words, shows them as a picture, and then makes you practise saying them the way you would in an interview. Everything is written in my own words and mapped to AWS.

Every lesson is laid out in the order an interview goes
  • In the interview: a 30-second answer to say aloud, then the follow-up questions with model answers you open after trying.
  • Hands-on: commands to copy and run against a POC in the repository, the output they really print, one thing to predict and check, and a short story to tell about it.
  • Check yourself: quick questions, the AWS version of the idea, and the takeaways.
  • Background: the chapter’s argument, diagrams and case studies, folded away at the bottom. Open it when an answer doesn’t make sense yet.

Short on time? Do the five interview drills first, then the hands-on SLO track. They cover the questions that come up most, and each ends in a lab you run yourself.

Mark a lesson complete at the bottom and its station on the route map fills in. Your progress is kept in this browser.

  • Do the exercise. Running the code is what makes the ideas stick.
